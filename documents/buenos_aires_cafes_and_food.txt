Minerva Student Reviews — Buenos Aires Cafes & Study Spots
=============================================================
Compiled from student WhatsApp groups, Discord, and anonymous surveys (2024–2025)

---

Café Tortoni (Avenida de Mayo 825, Monserrat)
Rating: ★★★☆☆ (for studying) / ★★★★★ (for experience)
"This is the most famous cafe in Buenos Aires — it's been open since 1858 and it's gorgeous inside. Marble tables, stained glass, old-world elegance. But for STUDYING? No. It's packed with tourists, there's no wifi, and a coffee is ARS $3,000 (~$3) which is expensive by Argentine standards. Go once to experience the history, take photos, then find a real study spot."
— M26

"Tortoni is a museum that happens to serve coffee. Beautiful, historic, terrible for productivity. Go on a Tuesday morning when it's less crowded. Skip the food — it's overpriced and mediocre."
— M25

---

LAB Training Center & Coffee (Humboldt 1542, Palermo)
Rating: ★★★★★
"THE study cafe in Buenos Aires. Fast wifi (they post the speed on the wall — usually 80-100mbps), tons of outlets, great espresso for ARS $2,000 (~$2), and a coworking vibe where everyone is on their laptops. Open until 8pm. The cold brew is excellent and they have a lunch menu with sandwiches for ARS $3,500 (~$3.50). This is where our Minerva study group meets every Tuesday."
— M26, Social Sciences

"LAB is the St. Oberholz of Buenos Aires. Reliable wifi, coffee culture, no one rushes you. The upstairs area is quieter. I wrote half my assignments here during the Buenos Aires rotation. Can't recommend it enough."
— M25

---

Birkin (Humboldt 1587, Palermo — right near LAB)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"A backup when LAB is full. Nice space, good coffee (flat white ARS $2,500 ~$2.50), wifi is decent. They have a nice patio area if the weather is good. The avocado toast is surprisingly good for ARS $4,000 (~$4). Gets more restaurant-vibes in the evening but during the day it's calm enough for work."
— M27

---

Biblioteca Nacional (National Library, Agüero 2502, Recoleta)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Free, beautiful brutalist building, quiet study rooms, wifi, and air conditioning. You need to register but it's free and quick. The reading rooms on the upper floors have stunning views of the park below. This is the best free study space in Buenos Aires. Open weekdays until 9pm, weekends until 5pm."
— M26

"The Biblioteca Nacional is an underrated gem. Most Minerva students don't even know about it. The architecture alone is worth the visit but the study facilities are genuinely excellent. Bring your own charger — outlets are limited in some rooms."
— Class of 2026

---

Cuervo Café (Uriarte 1567, Palermo Soho)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Great specialty coffee in Palermo. The wifi is good enough for regular work (not video calls). A cortado is ARS $1,800 (~$1.80) which is very fair. They roast their own beans and the baristas actually care about the craft. Small space — maybe 10 seats — so come early. Perfect for a 2-hour focused session."
— M25

=== BUENOS AIRES AFFORDABLE RESTAURANTS ===

Parrilla Don Julio (Guatemala 4691, Palermo — SPLURGE)
Rating: ★★★★★
"OK this is NOT cheap — a steak is ARS $15,000-25,000 (~$15-25). But it's the best steak restaurant in Buenos Aires and possibly the world (it's been ranked #1 in South America). If your family visits or you want ONE special meal, this is it. Reserve a week ahead."
— M26

---

Local Parrillas (neighborhood grill restaurants)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Skip Don Julio for weekly eating and find your neighborhood parrilla. Almost every block has one. A bife de chorizo (sirloin steak) with fries and salad is ARS $6,000-8,000 (~$6-8). Half the price of the fancy places and the quality is still incredible because THIS IS ARGENTINA — the beef is just good everywhere. Ask your Minerva RA for their favorite local spot."
— M25

"Our local parrilla in San Telmo became our group's living room. Steak, wine, and dessert for ARS $10,000/person (~$10). In what other city can you eat steak and drink Malbec for $10? Buenos Aires spoils you."
— M27

---

El Sanjuanino (Posadas 1515, Recoleta)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Empanadas for ARS $800 each (~$0.80)!! These are the best empanadas in the city — hand-folded, perfectly crispy, with generous filling. The carne (beef) and jamon y queso (ham and cheese) are my favorites. Get 4-5 empanadas for under $5 and you're completely full. They also have locro (corn stew) which is incredible in winter."
— M26

"El Sanjuanino is my #1 affordable eat in Buenos Aires. The empanadas are perfect. The humita (corn tamale) is also amazing. Cash only, always a short line, but it moves fast."
— Class of 2026

---

Mercado de San Telmo (Defensa 950, San Telmo)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Indoor food market with tons of options — empanadas, pizza, pasta, sushi, craft beer. Prices vary but you can eat well for ARS $4,000-6,000 (~$4-6). The choripán (sausage sandwich) vendors are the best deal. Also great for buying groceries — fresh produce, cheese, and dulce de leche. Go on Sundays when the outdoor antique market is also running."
— M27

BUDGET TIPS FROM MINERVA BUENOS AIRES STUDENTS:
- Argentina's economy means the official exchange rate vs. "blue dollar" rate matters — research this before arriving
- Medialunas (Argentine croissants) from any bakery: ARS $300-500 each (~$0.30-0.50) — the best breakfast
- Pizza by the slice (pizza al molde): ARS $800-1,500 for a massive slice. Buenos Aires pizza is thick and cheesy.
- Subte (subway) monthly pass is affordable — don't take taxis everywhere
- Wine is insanely cheap — a great bottle of Malbec from a wine shop is ARS $3,000-5,000 (~$3-5)
- AVOID: Restaurants in Puerto Madero — tourist trap with prices 3x normal
- AVOID: Any restaurant on Calle Florida — designed for tourists, terrible value
- Cook asado (Argentine BBQ) at home — buy meat at the local carnicería, way cheaper than restaurants
