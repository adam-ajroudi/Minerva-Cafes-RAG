Minerva Student Reviews — Taipei Cafes & Night Market Eats
=============================================================
Compiled from student LINE groups, Discord, and anonymous surveys (2024–2025)

---

Louisa Coffee (路易莎咖啡, multiple locations — try Zhongxiao or near NTU)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Taiwan's answer to Starbucks but actually GOOD for studying. An Americano is NT$65 (~$2 USD), wifi is fast and reliable, outlets at most seats, and they're open until 10-11pm. Some locations are 24 hours. The one near National Taiwan University is massive — two floors, quiet upstairs area, perfect for Minerva study sessions. This became my second home during Taipei rotation."
— M26

"Louisa is the best deal for a cafe study session anywhere in the world. $2 coffee, great wifi, no time limit, quiet atmosphere. I genuinely don't understand how they're profitable. Go to the ones near universities for the best study vibes."
— M25, Business concentration

---

SMooTH Coffee (中山區)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Cute specialty coffee shop with fast wifi and a chill atmosphere. Latte is NT$140 (~$4.30). Smaller space so it fills up on weekends but weekday mornings are perfect. The baristas are passionate about coffee and the pour-overs are excellent. Outlets at the bar seats. A hidden gem."
— M27

---

Cafe Junkies (永康街, near Dongmen station)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Right in the Yongkang Street area which is already amazing for food. Good wifi, decent seating, and their cold brew is addictive. NT$120 (~$3.70) for a cold brew. After your study session you can walk 2 minutes and get the world-famous Din Tai Fung xiaolongbao or mango shaved ice from Smoothie House. Location, location, location."
— M26

---

National Taiwan Library (Zhonghe)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Free, quiet, great wifi, air conditioned (crucial in Taipei summers). You need to register but it's free with any ID. They have private study rooms you can book and the main reading room has outlets at every desk. The only downside is it's a bit far from central Taipei — take the MRT to Sihzhong station. Worth the trip for serious study days."
— M25

---

Starbucks Reserve (Songshan Cultural Park)
Rating: ★★★☆☆
"Honestly? This one is more for the experience than for studying. The building is gorgeous — designed by Kengo Kuma — and the coffee is their premium Reserve line. But a latte is NT$200 (~$6) which is expensive for Taipei, seating is uncomfortable for long sessions, and it's always packed with tourists taking photos. Go once, take a picture, then study at Louisa."
— M26

=== TAIPEI NIGHT MARKET FOOD GUIDE ===

Shilin Night Market (士林夜市)
Rating: ★★★★★
"THE night market for first-timers. The food court underground is overwhelming in the best way. Must-try items:
- Oyster omelette (蚵仔煎): NT$60 (~$1.85) — crispy, savory, uniquely Taiwanese
- Stinky tofu (臭豆腐): NT$50 (~$1.55) — yes it smells terrible, yes it tastes amazing, just try it
- Giant fried chicken cutlet (雞排): NT$70 (~$2.15) — literally bigger than your head
- Bubble tea from any stall: NT$40-60 (~$1.25-1.85)
I could eat here every night for a month and not try everything. Budget NT$200-300 (~$6-9) for a full meal of multiple items."
— M26

"Shilin is tourist-heavy but the food is genuinely great. Go after 6pm when all the stalls are open. Bring cash — most vendors don't take cards. The pepper bun (胡椒餅) stall near the main entrance always has a line but it's worth the 10-minute wait."
— M25

---

Raohe Night Market (饒河夜市)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Smaller and more manageable than Shilin. The pepper buns at the entrance stall are LEGENDARY — NT$60 for a crispy, juicy pork bun straight from the clay oven. Also try the medicinal herbal stew (藥燉排骨, NT$70) which sounds weird but is deeply comforting. Less touristy than Shilin, more locals."
— M27

---

Ningxia Night Market (寧夏夜市)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Small but focused — mostly food, less trinkets. Famous for taro balls, oyster vermicelli, and lu rou fan (braised pork rice, NT$40 = $1.25). This is the market the locals go to. Everything is under NT$100 per item."
— M26

---

Din Tai Fung (鼎泰豐, various locations)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Not a night market but I have to mention it. The xiaolongbao (soup dumplings) here are world-famous for a reason. 10 pieces for NT$250 (~$7.70). Yes you can get cheaper XLB at night markets but Din Tai Fung is on another level. The Xinyi Road original location has the most history but also the longest wait. Use their app to get in line remotely."
— M26, originally from Hyderabad

BUDGET TIPS FROM MINERVA TAIPEI STUDENTS:
- Night markets are genuinely the cheapest way to eat well — NT$200 (~$6) is a full dinner
- Convenience stores (7-Eleven, FamilyMart) have AMAZING food — bento boxes for NT$70 (~$2), tea eggs for NT$10
- Taipei tap water is NOT drinkable — buy water or use filtered water stations
- Download the YouBike app — public bikes are almost free and the best way to get between night markets
- AVOID the restaurants directly in Taipei 101 mall — tourist prices, mediocre food
- Bento boxes from local shops (便當店) are NT$80-100 (~$2.50-3) for a full meal with rice, meat, and vegetables
