Minerva Student Reviews — Hyderabad Cafes, Restaurants & Food Guide
=====================================================================
Compiled from student WhatsApp groups, Discord, and anonymous surveys (2024–2025)

---

Roastery Coffee House (Jubilee Hills)
Rating: ★★★★★
"The best study cafe in Hyderabad. Period. Fast wifi (I've tested — consistently 50mbps+), outlets at every table, AC cranked up (essential in Hyderabad heat), and great coffee for ₹150-200 (~$1.80-2.40). Open until 11pm. The cappuccino is legit and they have good sandwiches too. I basically lived here during exam weeks."
— M26, Computational Sciences

"Roastery is where the Minerva study group meets every Thursday. Upstairs is quieter than downstairs. The cold coffee is my go-to order — ₹180 (~$2.15) and it's strong. They don't rush you out even if you sit for 4-5 hours. Best study cafe experience in any Minerva city honestly."
— M25

---

Autumn Leaf Café (Banjara Hills)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Charming little cafe with books everywhere. Wifi is decent (not blazing but fine for regular work), there are outlets but you might need to grab a seat near the wall. The atmosphere is cozy and quiet. Their banana walnut cake is amazing. Coffee is ₹120-180 (~$1.45-2.15). Good for 2-3 hour afternoon sessions."
— M27

---

Lamakaan (Banjara Hills)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"This is a cultural space / open cafe that hosts events, poetry readings, and art shows. During the day it's quiet enough for work with free wifi. The food is homestyle and cheap — thali meals for ₹150 (~$1.80). It's also a great way to meet locals and learn about Hyderabad's art scene. Not the best for hardcore study sessions but perfect for lighter work."
— M26

---

Indian Coffee House (Multiple locations, try the one in Abids)
Rating: ★★★★★
"An institution. Filter coffee for ₹30 (~$0.36). Yes, THIRTY RUPEES. The atmosphere is old-school — ceiling fans, waiters in white uniforms, students and professors debating at tables. Wifi is nonexistent and that's actually the point — go here to read, write, or think without distractions. Every intellectual in Hyderabad has sat at these tables."
— M25

"The Indian Coffee House is a Hyderabad essential. Don't go for wifi or outlets. Go for the ₹30 filter coffee, the dosa for ₹50, and the feeling of sitting in a place with decades of history. Then go to Roastery for your actual study session."
— Class of 2026

=== HYDERABAD FOOD — THE BIRYANI CAPITAL ===

Paradise Biryani (Multiple locations, try the original in Secunderabad)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Paradise is the most famous biryani in Hyderabad and for good reason. The chicken biryani is ₹280 (~$3.35) and the portion is MASSIVE. Fragrant, spiced perfectly, with tender meat. The Secunderabad original location has the most history but any branch is good. This is the dish you have to eat in Hyderabad."
— M26

"I eat Paradise biryani at least once a week. It's cheap, it's filling, and it's genuinely some of the best food I've had anywhere. The veg biryani (₹200) is also excellent for non-meat-eaters. Always get the mirchi ka salan (chili curry) on the side — it's the perfect complement."
— M27

---

Bawarchi (RTC X Roads)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Locals will tell you Bawarchi is better than Paradise and honestly? They might be right. The biryani is slightly spicier and more aromatic. Chicken biryani is ₹250 (~$3). There's always a line out the door at lunch and dinner which tells you everything. Cash only at the counter — they're fast though."
— M26, originally from Buenos Aires

"The Bawarchi vs. Paradise debate is the great divide of Minerva Hyderabad students. I'm Team Bawarchi — the flavor is deeper, the rice is more fragrant, and it's ₹30 cheaper. Fight me."
— M25

---

Ram Ki Bandi (Night-only dosa cart, near Mozamjahi Market, Nampally)
Rating: ★★★★★
"This is a DOSA CART that only operates from midnight to 5am and there's a LINE around the block. The butter dosa is ₹80 (~$0.96) and it is hands down the best dosa I've ever eaten. The butter literally pools on the dosa. Go at 1am with your Minerva crew — it's a rite of passage. Cash only."
— M26

"Ram Ki Bandi midnight dosa run is the Hyderabad equivalent of El Farolito in SF. You haven't done the Minerva Hyderabad experience until you've stood in line at 2am for a ₹80 dosa that makes you question every dosa you've eaten before."
— Class of 2027

---

Shah Ghouse (Tolichowki)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Late night biryani and kebabs. Open until 1am. The mutton biryani is ₹350 (~$4.20) and portions are absurd. The haleem during Ramadan season is legendary. This is where you go after a late study session — the pathar ka gosht (stone-cooked meat) is incredible."
— M26

BUDGET TIPS FROM MINERVA HYDERABAD STUDENTS:
- Street food is safe and delicious — follow the crowds to find the best stalls
- ₹100 (~$1.20) can get you a full meal at local tiffin centers (breakfast joints)
- Idli, dosa, and vada at any local joint: ₹30-50 for a filling breakfast
- Chai from street vendors: ₹10-15 per glass. Drink it constantly.
- Auto-rickshaws: always negotiate price BEFORE getting in, or insist on meter
- AVOID: Western food chains in malls — overpriced and worse than what you'd get at home
- Cook at home using vegetables from Rythu Bazaar — absurdly cheap fresh produce
- Swiggy and Zomato delivery are affordable here — unlike in the US, delivery fees are reasonable
