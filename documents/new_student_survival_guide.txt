Minerva Student Survival Guide — Food & Cafe Tips for New Students
=====================================================================
Written collaboratively by Class of 2025 and 2026 students for incoming M27 cohort

---

BEFORE YOU ARRIVE AT ANY CITY:

1. Download the local food delivery app BEFORE you arrive:
   - SF: DoorDash / UberEats (but avoid using them — fees are insane)
   - Seoul: Baemin (배달의민족) — delivery is affordable here!
   - Taipei: Foodpanda or UberEats
   - Hyderabad: Swiggy or Zomato — delivery fees are very reasonable
   - Berlin: Lieferando — delivery fees are moderate
   - Buenos Aires: Rappi or PedidosYa

2. Learn these food phrases in the local language:
   - Korean: "이거 주세요" (igeo juseyo) = "This one please" (just point and say this)
   - Mandarin: "这个" (zhège) = "This one" (same strategy)
   - Hindi: "Kitna hai?" = "How much is it?"
   - German: "Ich hätte gerne..." = "I would like..."
   - Spanish: "¿Cuánto cuesta?" = "How much does it cost?"

FIRST WEEK FOOD STRATEGY (for each city):

San Francisco:
"Your first week, stock up on groceries at Trader Joe's. DO NOT eat out every meal — $15/meal x 3 meals x 7 days = $315/week, which is insane. Cook breakfast, pack lunch, eat out for dinner maybe 3 times. Hit El Farolito and Señor Sisig early so you know where to go when you're craving something amazing and cheap."
— M25

Seoul:
"First thing: find your nearest Kimbap Cheonguk and convenience store. Those two will keep you alive and happy on ₩15,000/day (~$11). Explore night markets and BBQ spots with your cohort — Korean food is best shared in groups. The university cafeterias near campus serve full meals for ₩4,000-5,000."
— M26

Taipei:
"Hit a night market on your first night. You'll realize food in Taipei is incredibly cheap and delicious. Stock up at FamilyMart for breakfasts (their onigiri are ₩35/NT$35 and good). Louisa Coffee will be your study spot — find the one nearest your residence immediately."
— M25

Hyderabad:
"Try biryani within the first 48 hours — either Paradise or Bawarchi. Your life will change. Get comfortable with spicy food because EVERYTHING is spicy (and delicious). The tiffin centers near your residence serve unlimited breakfast (idli, dosa, vada, coffee) for ₹100. That's $1.20 for unlimited food. Hyderabad is the cheapest city and the food is arguably the best."
— M27

Berlin:
"Get a döner kebab on day one. Then find St. Oberholz for studying. Stock up on groceries at Aldi or Lidl — you can eat well for €30/week if you cook. The Turkish grocery stores have incredible produce. Berlin's cafe culture is perfect for studying — enjoy it."
— M26

Buenos Aires:
"Argentine dinner starts at 9pm. Adjust your schedule. Your first meal should be empanadas from any local spot — they're cheap and delicious. Find your neighborhood parrilla for steak nights. Wine is cheaper than water here (not literally but almost). The medialunas from any bakery are the perfect breakfast for $0.50 each."
— M25

DIETARY RESTRICTIONS — CITY-BY-CITY GUIDE:

Vegetarian:
- EASIEST: Hyderabad (huge vegetarian culture, clearly labeled), Taipei (Buddhist vegetarian restaurants everywhere)
- MODERATE: Berlin (good veggie options at most cafes), Buenos Aires (challenging but improving)
- HARDEST: Seoul (lots of hidden meat/fish in sauces — ask carefully), San Francisco (easy in theory but expensive)

Halal:
- EASIEST: Hyderabad (majority halal), Berlin (Turkish neighborhoods are fully halal)
- MODERATE: Seoul (Itaewon has halal options), Taipei (a few halal restaurants near Taipei Grand Mosque)
- HARDEST: Buenos Aires (very limited), San Francisco (available but scattered)

Gluten-free:
- EASIEST: Seoul (rice-based cuisine), Hyderabad (rice and lentil based)
- MODERATE: Taipei (rice-heavy but soy sauce contains wheat), San Francisco (lots of GF options but expensive)
- HARDEST: Berlin (bread capital of the world), Buenos Aires (empanadas, pizza, pasta everywhere)

THE "NEVER DO THIS" LIST (from students who learned the hard way):

"Never order delivery in San Francisco. A $12 burrito becomes $25 after fees, tip, and service charges. Just walk to the restaurant."
— M26

"Never eat in a tourist area in any city. Prices are 2-3x normal and food quality is worse. In Berlin, avoid Alexanderplatz. In Taipei, avoid the area right around Taipei 101. In Buenos Aires, avoid Puerto Madero."
— M25

"Never assume tap water is drinkable. It's fine in SF, Berlin, and Seoul. Drink filtered/bottled in Taipei, Hyderabad, and Buenos Aires."
— M27

"Never skip breakfast to save money. In Hyderabad and Taipei, breakfast is the cheapest meal of the day (under $1.50). In Seoul, convenience store breakfast is under $3. Skipping it just makes you spend more on a big lunch."
— M26

"Never go to a night market in Taipei on a full stomach. You WILL regret not having room for pepper buns, stinky tofu, oyster omelettes, and bubble tea."
— Class of 2026
