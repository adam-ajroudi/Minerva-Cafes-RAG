Minerva Student Reviews — Seoul Affordable Restaurants & Street Food
======================================================================
Compiled from student KakaoTalk groups, Discord, and anonymous surveys (2024–2025)

---

Kimbap Cheonguk (김밥천국, "Kimbap Heaven" — multiple locations everywhere)
Rating: ★★★★★
"This chain is the backbone of student eating in Korea. Kimbap rolls for ₩3,500 (~$2.60), bibimbap for ₩5,000 (~$3.75), ramyeon for ₩3,000 (~$2.25). Everything is fresh, fast, and insanely cheap. There's one within walking distance of literally any location in Seoul. Open early, closes late. This is where I ate 4 days a week minimum."
— M26

"I cannot overstate how important Kimbap Cheonguk is to surviving Seoul on a student budget. The tuna kimbap is my order — ₩3,500 for a full roll that keeps you going for hours. Some locations also do fried rice and stews for under ₩5,000. Absolute legend."
— M25

---

Gwangjang Market (광장시장, Jongno)
Rating: ★★★★★
"The market is a must-visit — it's been around since 1905 and the food is INCREDIBLE. Bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes) for ₩3,000, bibimbap for ₩6,000, tteokbokki for ₩4,000. Find the stall run by the grandmother who's been there for 40 years — her mayak kimbap (mini seaweed rolls) are famous and only ₩3,000 for like 20 pieces."
— M27

"Gwangjang Market is the best food experience in Seoul, full stop. Go hungry. Start with the knife-cut noodles (kalguksu ₩7,000), then the bindaetteok, then the raw beef yukhoe if you're adventurous. You'll spend under ₩15,000 (~$11) and eat like royalty. It's touristy but the food quality hasn't dropped."
— Class of 2026

---

Myeongdong Kyoja (명동교자, Myeongdong)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Their kalguksu (knife-cut noodle soup) is ₩10,000 (~$7.50) and it comes with free mandu (dumplings) on the side. The noodles are handmade, the broth is rich and clear. You WILL wait in line (15-30 min) but it moves fast. There's a reason this place has been packed since the 1960s. Skip the rest of Myeongdong's overpriced food and come here."
— M26

---

BBQ at Local 고깃집 (Korean BBQ restaurants, Mapo-gu area)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Korean BBQ can be cheap if you know where to go!! Skip the fancy places in Gangnam. The neighborhood samgyeopsal (pork belly) spots in Mapo-gu charge ₩9,000-12,000 per person for unlimited pork belly with all the banchan (side dishes). Get 3-4 Minerva friends together and it's an amazing group dinner for under $10/person. Look for places with 삼겹살 signs and no English menus — that's how you know it's legit."
— M25

"Our go-to BBQ spot was near Hapjeong station — the owner gave us extra banchan because we came so often. ₩10,000/person for all-you-can-eat samgyeopsal. Wrap the meat in lettuce with garlic and ssamjang sauce. This is peak Seoul eating and it doesn't have to be expensive."
— M26

---

Noryangjin Fish Market (노량진 수산시장)
Rating: ★★★★☆
"Adventurous but SO worth it. You pick live seafood from the market stalls downstairs, negotiate a price, then take it upstairs to a restaurant that prepares it for you (they charge ₩5,000-10,000 for preparation). I had a full sashimi platter for ₩20,000 (~$15) that would have been ₩50,000+ at a restaurant. Go with friends to split."
— M27

---

Tosokchon Samgyetang (토속촌삼계탕, near Gyeongbokgung)
Rating: ★★★★★
"Ginseng chicken soup that will cure anything — hangovers, homesickness, seasonal depression. ₩16,000 (~$12) for a whole chicken stuffed with rice, ginseng, and dates in a milky broth. It's pricier than street food but worth it as a treat. The line can be 30+ minutes during lunch — go at 2pm."
— M26

BUDGET TIPS FROM MINERVA SEOUL STUDENTS:
- Convenience stores (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven) have amazing prepared meals for ₩3,000-5,000
- Triangle kimbap (삼각김밥) from any convenience store: ₩1,200 snack that actually fills you up
- University cafeterias (학식) near Yonsei/Ewha: ₩4,000-5,000 for a full Korean meal, open to public
- Download Baemin (배달의민족) app for food delivery — look for free delivery promotions
- AVOID eating in Itaewon — prices are 2-3x normal Korean prices for mediocre food
- Cook at home using ingredients from Emart or Homeplus — Korean groceries are cheap
